# Fixed-work scaling method

1. Initial 28-seed/two-hand calibration aborted when projected completion exceeded the requested ten-minute configuration bound; no throughput estimate from its partial subset is used for worker selection.
2. Smaller calibration: 14 fixed seed clusters 108101–108114, one hand per match, two arms, both pool strata, 6/7/8 seats, all rotations: 1,176 physical hands/configuration. Run at 1,4,8,10,14 workers. All repetitions are explicitly reused inspected engineering seeds. No profit analysis or inference from calibration.
3. Use short-run throughput only to choose a fixed work count targeting 570 seconds (9.5 minutes) of sustained work at each worker count, allowing 10 minutes hard cap. The fixed count is written before launch and never extended based on profit or significance. Thermal warning status and worker RSS are saved. Idle at least 60 seconds between configurations. Runtime cap is a technical failure, retained in full.
4. Ten minutes of predicted work is not guaranteed ten minutes actual with fixed work. Select on completed sustained work, not a short-run burst; disclose duration and cap censoring. If a full ten-minute sustained ranking cannot be completed under this strict cap, leave selection provisional and do not call it a validated ten-minute winner.
5. Main variance pilot uses a conservative sustained rate, at most 90 minutes predicted work in one <=2-hour run, fresh seeds reserved only after the work count and source are fixed.

pmset -g therm reports warning/performance status when available. Numeric CPU temperature is not exposed by this interface; absence of a warning is not evidence of a particular temperature. Peak worker RSS is reported by process.resourceUsage().maxRSS (KiB converted to bytes).

Ruling: use a 9.5-minute prediction with the required hard 10-minute configuration cap. This permits honest fixed-work completion without intentionally manufacturing capped runs to simulate a time-based benchmark. Actual duration and any shortfall from a full ten-minute validation are explicit limitations; do not describe a shorter completed run as ten minutes.

Additional cost check (prespecified before main pilot): after choosing workers, reserve fresh scaling-only seeds at 115001 onward; adjustAllIn=false, same pool/arms/seats/rotations/hands. Fix count to at most 540 seconds predicted at the measured adjusted rate (raw-only should be cheaper), cap 600 seconds. Do not analyze profits. Use the measured raw-only throughput separately in raw power scenarios; private traces and all other overhead remain included. Then cool before the one fresh 840-cluster-or-smaller variance pilot at 120001 onward.

Prespecified treatment of a sustained benchmark hitting its 600-second resource cap: keep its profit data incomplete/inconclusive and do not use it for any return/variance estimate. A separate throughput lower bound may count only fully completed seed clusters divided by the entire observed capped wall time; discard all in-flight work from the numerator while retaining its cost in the denominator. This is an engineering capacity bound, not a favorable outcome subset. Label it capped/lower-bound, never a completed fixed-work result. It can support a conservative pilot runtime projection; prefer complete sustained results when rates are indistinguishable. No benchmark extends beyond its fixed count or cap, and no profit outcomes are inspected for this decision.

Candidate validation rule, set before the 10/14-worker sustained results: if the fastest selected configuration's completed sustained run is shorter than 540 seconds, perform one additional fresh fixed-work validation targeting 570 seconds at its observed rate (600-second cap). Use the slower rate of its sustained and validation runs for pilot planning. This prevents selecting solely on a short burst. Continue to report actual duration; never claim exactly ten minutes if shorter. No profit outcomes are used, no repeating until a favorable rate.
